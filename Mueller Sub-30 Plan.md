# Mueller Sub-30 Plan

## Goal and what your data says

You need to cut about 4:10 from your 2026 best of 34:05 on the Mueller Elephant Rock race segment to get under 30:00 by May. You have done it twice (27:51 in 2016, 29:43 in 2020), so the target is realistic, but it is a 12% gain and needs steady work for about 30 weeks.

- **Riding more is not the lever.** Yearly ride hours show almost no link to climb time (correlation +0.12). 2018 had 103 ride hours and a 35:15; 2016 had 88 and a 27:51.
- **Running is the strongest signal.** Run miles correlate at -0.77 with climb time. Your three sub-30 years had 43, 121 and 152 run miles, and no year with fewer than 40 run miles beat 32:51. 2026 has 6 run miles so far.
- **Gym work is a plausible helper, not proven.** 2020 had 26 gym-type sessions and a 29:43, but 2016 had 3 and was faster. Treat strength as support for the running and climbing, not the main driver.
- **Your 2020 peak had a pattern.** Six months of 2-3 rides a week, 9 to 13 rides a month from April to June, many with 1,200 to 1,600 ft, plus a January restart of running and gym work.

**Power target.** From your Sep 28 test (313 W max aerobic power, 235 W FTP estimate, 188 lb), a 29:59 needs about 198 W for 30 minutes, or 2.32 W/kg. Your 34:05 equals about 173 W. At 175 lb the target falls to about 187 W. The test says you may already have the capacity, so the March 30-minute trainer test settles whether the gap is power or climbing on the trail.

This rests on 11 yearly data points, so the plan follows standard training principles and uses your data to choose where to put the limited sessions: running and strength first, with focused climbing work added closer to May.

## Sessions

**Volt (track and field distance plan, 2 days a week).** Switch to this plan on Oct 19, after the family trip. Do 2 total-body sessions a week, Monday and Thursday until Jan 31. From Feb 1, do one moderate session a week on Tuesday, the day after Monday's quality ride. Keep at least one a week all year, including race season. This is the muscular endurance phase. Volt's heavier max strength and power phases come later, and the cycling studies that showed benefits used heavy lifts (80% or more of a one-rep max). For the first two weeks use about 80% of your usual leg weights and stop each set 2 to 3 reps short. Do the days in order and log the exercise names, sets, reps and weights so progress can be tracked. Alternate non-competing pairs (for example a split squat with a single-leg RDL) with 60 to 90 seconds rest. Add 5 lb when the last two reps feel easy and your form is clean. Keep jump lunges and other explosive work for fresh days, never the day before the long ride. Add 2 to 3 core stability exercises (planks, side planks, dead bugs) at the end of each session, since the old cycling program had more core work.

**Runs.** Easy means you could hold a conversation. Weeks 1 and 2 are 1 minute run, 1 minute walk for 20 minutes. Add 5 minutes a week to the longer run, never more than about 10% more weekly mileage. From Phase 2, add 4 to 6 strides (15 sec fast, 45 sec easy). Indoor track is fine. Skip the run if your calves or quads are tender. Target roughly 150 to 170 miles from October to May.

**Rides.** Ride by feel and heart rate, since there is no power meter. Sweet spot is hard but steady, about 7 out of 10. Climb pace is the effort you could hold for about 25 minutes, and it should land near 190 to 200 W by the March test. Trainer rides count as aerobic work. Track Saturday's outdoor climbing feet separately.

**Sweet spot versus threshold.** Sweet spot is 88 to 94% of FTP (about 205 to 220 W at 235 W) in blocks of 15 to 30 minutes, and you should finish able to keep going. Threshold is 95 to 105% (about 225 to 245 W) in blocks of 8 to 15 minutes, and you finish ready to stop. Heart rate runs close for both because it flattens near FTP, so only about 3 to 6 bpm separates them. Use watts and breathing instead. Sweet spot builds aerobic efficiency for long rides and costs about a day of recovery. Threshold raises FTP and race pace and costs one to two days.

**Saturday long ride.** Mostly easy. Ride by heart rate (112 to 136 bpm), since there is no power meter outdoors. From March, most Saturdays are two 8 to 10 minute climb-pace efforts at heart rate 155 to 165 on Farmington Canyon Road or another steady climb, with a Farm time trial at race pace on Mar 13 and a full-effort Farmington Canyon Road climb on Apr 10 (Mueller is closed until about May). The cross country surge ride is left out of the schedule because Thursday now carries more VO2 work; it stays in the library as an optional swap on a week with no Thursday VO2. Eat and drink on rides over 90 minutes.

**Weight.** Aim for 175 to 180 lb by May through normal eating, no more than about 0.5 lb a week. Each 10 lb lower cuts the power needed by about 6 to 7 W.

**Asthma.** Your Oct 5 time trial ended early when it flared. Warm up fully before hard efforts and raise the interval plan below with your doctor before the first hard session. The sweet spot from Nov 18 and the VO2 sessions from Feb 11 need the most care. If it flares, drop back to easy riding for a week before trying again, and tell your doctor if it keeps happening.

How to use them:

- Watts come first on the trainer. Heart rate confirms the work. It does not set it.
- Heart rate lags 1 to 2 minutes at the start of an interval and drifts up 5 to 10 bpm over a long ride. Trust it for blocks of 8 minutes or more, and judge shorter efforts by feel and breathing.
- Z4 is wide (155 to 172), so it covers sweet spot, threshold and early VO2 work. Tell them apart by watts and how long you can hold the effort.
- You rarely reach Z5. Your hardest rides peak at 174 to 178 bpm, so a good VO2 session will mostly show as Z4. That is normal.
- Threshold blocks at 170+ bpm for the same watts point to fatigue, heat or an asthma flare, not extra fitness.
- Strava's default zones put Z2 at 120 to 148 and Z5 at 179+. Both are off for you, which is why the custom zones are worth setting.
- Recheck FTP and LTHR after the Jan 16 ramp test and the March 4 test, and update this table if they change by more than a few percent.

## Interval workouts

Every workout is built the same way: a warm-up, a main set of repeated work and recovery steps, and a cool-down. Work targets are a percent of FTP, with watts calculated from your current FTP. Heart rate is a check against the zone table above.

### How to read these workouts

- **FTP is 235 W.** Watts = FTP x percent, rounded to the nearest 5 W. When the March 4 test moves FTP by more than a few percent, recompute every watt target from its percent.
- **Time:** minutes in the tables. Weekday rides total 60 minutes or less. Only Saturday goes longer.
- **Heart rate:** a check, not a target. It lags 1 to 2 minutes into each rep. The ranges are estimates until you have logged a few sessions of each type.
- **Cadence** is in rpm. Recovery steps use any cadence. **RPE** is effort on a 1 to 10 scale.
- **Progression:** go to the next row only if you finished every rep at target watts. If not, repeat the row. Never add reps and raise watts in the same week.
- **Deload weeks** (weeks 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24 and 28) use the shorter row in the schedule, at the same watts.

Stop rules. Log which one fired:

- Wheeze, chest tightness or unusual breathlessness: stop hard work, ride easy, and do no more hard efforts that day.
- Power more than 5% below target for 2 minutes in a row: end the block, take the full recovery, and do another block only at target.
- Heart rate above 172 bpm in the first half of a sweet spot or threshold block at target watts: stop the set. It points to fatigue, heat or illness.
- Leg soreness of 5 out of 10 or more that morning: swap the session for E1 (the easy ride below).

Outdoors without watts, use the heart rate and RPE columns and the time targets. On Saturday, use the heart rate range for the effort (155 to 165 bpm for RP1).

- If your legs are sore or you're run-down, swap a quality ride for an easy one instead of pushing through.
- If you're sick or your asthma flares, cut intensity for a few days. Missing a session costs less than an illness that drags on.
